★Cheat sheet@@cheat@@

CHAPTER 1 · Digital data

  • Data = quantities, characters, symbols on which a computer operates; stored/transmitted as electrical signals.
  • Data → Information → Insights. Data is internal & external; homogeneous & heterogeneous.
  • 3 types: Structured (rows/cols, RDBMS) · Semi-structured (tags, self-describing: XML, JSON, HTML, email) · Unstructured (no model: images, video, chats) = 80–90%.
  • Cardinality = no. of rows; Degree = no. of columns.
  • Constraints: UNIQUE, NOT NULL, business rule, permissible values; PRIMARY KEY; tables related by common column.
  • Ease of structured (ISIST): Insert/update/delete (DML) · Security (encryption, tokenization) · Indexing · Scalability (scale up) · Transactions (ACID).
  • ACID: Atomicity (all or nothing) · Consistency (consistent state) · Isolation (as if alone) · Durability (permanent).
  • Semi-structured: no data model; tags segregate semantics & enforce hierarchy; no data/schema separation; same-class entities may differ in attributes/order. Chars: inconsistent structure, self-describing label/value, schema blended, unknown attributes.
  • XML–SOAP, JSON–REST; MongoDB & Couchbase/CouchDB store JSON natively.
  • Unstructured: machine-generated (satellite, atmospheric, radar, CCTV, medical imaging) vs human-generated (emails, office docs, texts, audio, social media). Terminology issues: implied structure; structure not helpful; unanticipated structure.
  • 7 techniques: Data mining (KDD; association rules, regression, collaborative filtering) · Text analytics (categorisation, clustering, sentiment, entity extraction) · NLP · Noisy text analytics · Manual tagging with metadata · POS tagging · UIMA (IBM, open source).
  • Comparison (7 props): Technology · Transactions · Versioning · Flexibility · Scalability · Robustness · Query performance.

CHAPTER 2 · Big Data

  • 3 Cs of data: Composition (structure, sources, granularity, types, static/streaming) · Condition (usable as-is or needs cleansing?) · Context (where/why generated).
  • Evolution: ≤1970s mainframes (primitive, structured) → 1980s–90s RDBMS (complex, relational) → 2000s+ WWW & IoT (complex, unstructured; data-driven).
  • Gartner: "high-volume, high-velocity and high-variety information assets that demand cost-effective, innovative forms of information processing for enhanced insight and decision making."
  • Data → Information → Actionable intelligence → Better decisions → Enhanced business value.
  • 6 challenges: growth, cloud & virtualisation, retention, lack of skills, capture/storage/processing/security, visualisation. (Fig: capture, storage, curation, search, analysis, transfer, visualisation, privacy.)
  • 3 Vs: Volume (KB→YB, ×1024) · Velocity (batch → periodic → near real time → real time) · Variety (structured, semi, unstructured).
  • Sources: internal (file systems, SQL, NoSQL, archives) · external (public web) · both (sensors, logs, social, business apps, media, docs).
  • Other Vs: Veracity (trust) & Validity (correctness) · Volatility (how long valid) · Variability (inconsistent flow, peaks).
  • Why: more data → accurate analysis → confidence → efficiency: cost ↓, time ↓, new products, smart decisions.
  • BI vs Big Data: central server vs distributed FS · vertical vs horizontal scaling · offline vs real-time+offline · structured vs variety · data→code vs code→data · schema-on-write vs schema-on-read.

CHAPTER 3 · Big Data Analytics

  • Key idea: large data → analyse → patterns → better decisions (Amazon recommendations, 500-truck example).
  • Pyramid: Data (collect) → Information (organise) → Knowledge (summarise, analyse) → Actionable insights (synthesise) → Decision.
  • What BDA is (6): technology-enabled · deeper insights · competitive edge · handshake of IT + data scientists + business users · large varied data · moving code to data.
  • Isn't: only volume · just technology · replace RDBMS · replace DW · only for Google/Amazon · one-size-fits-all RDBMS.
  • Hype: data growth · cheaper storage · tools available.
  • 1st school: basic · operationalised · advanced · monetised. 2nd school: 1.0 descriptive (1950s–2009) · 2.0 +predictive (2005–2012, Hadoop) · 3.0 +prescriptive (2012–, in-memory, in-DB, ML). Hindsight → insight → foresight.
  • 7 top challenges: scale · security · schema · continuous availability · consistency · partition tolerance · data quality.
  • Importance — 4 approaches: reactive BI · reactive BDA · proactive analytics · proactive BDA ★.
  • Data science = extracting knowledge from data; skills: business acumen + technology + mathematics. Process: collect → prepare → analyse → show → communicate → decide.
  • Data scientist (4): data management · analytical techniques · business/domain expertise · communication & presentation.
  • In-memory: data in RAM. In-database: compute inside DB (OLTP → ETL → EDW).
  • SMP: shared memory, one OS, tightly coupled, caches + system bus. MPP: own memory + OS per processor, messaging, harder to program.
  • Parallel = tightly coupled, user sees one system. Distributed = loosely coupled machines over network.
  • SM / SD / SN; SN advantages: fault isolation + scalability.
  • CAP (Brewer): pick two of C, A, P. CA: RDBMS · CP: HBase, MongoDB, Redis, MemcacheDB, BigTable · AP: Riak, Cassandra, CouchDB, Dynamo.

🎯Important exam questions@@imp@@

Short answers (2–3 marks)

  1. Define data. Differentiate quantitative and qualitative data. §1.1
  2. What is cardinality and degree of a relation? §1.3
  3. Expand and explain ACID. §1.3
  4. Why is semi-structured data called self-describing? §1.4
  5. Why is an email placed in the unstructured category? §1.8
  6. What is UIMA? Who developed it? §1.6
  7. What is noisy text? Give two examples. §1.6
  8. State the Gartner definition of Big Data. §2.3
  9. Differentiate veracity, volatility and variability. §2.6
  10. What is schema-on-read? §2.8
  11. What is meant by "moving code to data"? §3.2
  12. List what Big Data Analytics isn't. §3.2
  13. What is in-memory analytics? Advantages? §3.8
  14. Differentiate SMP and MPP. §3.8
  15. State the CAP theorem. §3.9

Long answers (5–10 marks)

  1. Explain the classification of digital data with examples and a neat diagram. §1.2–1.5
  2. Explain structured data and the ease of working with it. §1.3
  3. Write a note on semi-structured and unstructured data. §1.4–1.5
  4. Explain the techniques to find patterns in / interpret unstructured data. §1.6
  5. Compare structured, semi-structured and unstructured data. §1.7
  6. Explain the evolution and definition of Big Data. §2.2–2.3
  7. Explain the 3 Vs of Big Data with sources of data. §2.5
  8. What are the challenges with Big Data? §2.4
  9. Differentiate traditional BI and Big Data. §2.8
  10. What is Big Data Analytics? Explain with a diagram. §3.2
  11. Explain Analytics 1.0, 2.0 and 3.0. §3.3
  12. Explain the top challenges facing Big Data. §3.4
  13. Why is Big Data Analytics important? (4 approaches) §3.5
  14. Explain data science and the skill sets of a data scientist. §3.6–3.7
  15. Explain shared-nothing architecture and its advantages. §3.8
  16. Explain the CAP theorem with an example and the CA/CP/AP databases. §3.9
🎯 How to write a 10-mark answer
  1. Definition (use the exact textbook words — the 🔑 / Definition boxes).
  2. Diagram — neat, labelled, with a figure caption. Draw it early in the answer.
  3. Explanation in points — one heading per point, 2–3 lines each.
  4. Example — Amazon, bank, student marks, truck company… examiners love examples.
  5. Conclusion — 1 line tying back to "better decisions" / "business value".
