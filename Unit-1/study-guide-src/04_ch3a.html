3.1Understanding Big Data Analytics through examples@@s3-1@@

🛒 Scenario 1 — Amazon recommendation system

You register on Amazon and buy a book by your favourite author. Soon Amazon recommends: books by the same author, books on similar topics, products based on your previous purchases, and products bought by customers with similar interests.

How? Amazon collects and analyses your past purchases, browsing history, preferences and buying patterns.

🚚 Scenario 2 — Truck transport company

You own 500 trucks. A customer asks for an urgent delivery and will pay double. Which truck should you send?

You must analyse: current truck location, available load capacity, fuel consumption, traffic on routes, distance to customer, expected delivery time.

Key idea of Big Data Analytics

Where do we begin with Big Data Analytics?

Big Data Analytics starts with raw data. Raw data by itself may not be very useful — we process it step by step until it is useful for decisions:

  1. Collecting → DATA: collect raw data from different sources.
  2. Organising → INFORMATION: classify and organise data so it becomes meaningful.
  3. Summarising & Analysing → KNOWLEDGE: find patterns, trends and relationships.
  4. Synthesising → ACTIONABLE INSIGHTS: decide what useful action can be taken.
  5. DECISION MAKING: use the insights to make a better and faster decision.

3.2What is Big Data Analytics?@@s3-2@@

🗣 In simple words
Big Data Analytics (BDA) = using tools to dig through huge, varied, fast data to find patterns that help a business decide better and faster than competitors.
Figure 3.3 — What is Big Data Analytics?

Your slides explain 6 points:

1. Technology-enabled analytics

Many analytics and visualisation tools are available to process huge data — from IBM, Tableau, SAS, R Analytics, statistical tools, WPS (World Programming Systems).

2. Getting deeper, meaningful insights

Having data is not enough — we need insight. Helps a company understand: who its customers are, what they like and buy, what else can be sold to them (cross-sell), how to use vendors and suppliers effectively.

3. Gives a competitive edge

Quicker and better decisions than competitors. Company A analyses customer behaviour and offers the right products early; Company B doesn't → A wins customers.
Better analysis → Faster decisions → Competitive advantage.

4. A tight handshake between three communities

IT team — provides computers, databases, networks, infrastructure.
Data scientists — analyse data and discover patterns.
Business users — use the results to make business decisions.

5. Working with very large & varied datasets

Volume (huge quantity) and variety (customer records + images + videos + emails + social posts + transactions) may exceed the capability of traditional systems.

6. Moving code to data

Program = a few KB/MB; data = TB/PB. It's easier to move the small program to where the data is stored than to move huge data to the program. Processing happens close to the data.

📈 1. Huge growth of data

Data is exploding from web, mobiles, sensors, social media.

💾 2. Storage has become cheaper

Storing TBs/PBs is now affordable (commodity hardware, cloud).

Open-source and commercial tools (Hadoop, R, Tableau…) are easily available.

3.3Classification of analytics@@s3-3@@

There are basically two schools of thought:

First school of thought — 4 types
  1. Basic analytics: slicing and dicing of data for basic business insights — reporting on historical data, basic visualisation, etc.
  2. Operationalised analytics: analytics that gets woven into the enterprise's business processes.
  3. Advanced analytics: forecasting the future using predictive and prescriptive modelling.
  4. Monetised analytics: analytics used to derive direct business revenue.
Second school of thought — 3 generations

Analytics 1.0 → Analytics 2.0 → Analytics 3.0

Each generation adds a new type of analytics on top of the previous one:

1.0 = Descriptive
2.0 = Descriptive + Predictive
3.0 = Descriptive + Predictive + Prescriptive

🧠 Memory trick — first school
"BOAM" — Basic, Operationalised, Advanced, Monetised (from looking back → making money).

3.4Top challenges facing Big Data@@s3-4@@

📏 1. Scale

How to store and manage the huge and continuously growing amount of data.

🔐 2. Security

How to protect Big Data from unauthorised access and attacks.

🧬 3. Schema

Rigid schemas have no place in Big Data — we need dynamic schemas.

🕐 4. Continuous availability

Systems must give 24/7 support because downtime is a problem.

🎯 5. Consistency

Make sure users see correct and updated data.

🧱 6. Partition tolerant

How to build systems that keep working despite hardware and software failures.

✨ 7. Data quality

How to maintain data accuracy, completeness, timeliness, and proper metadata.

🧠 Memory trick — 7 top challenges
"Six Students Sat Calmly, Copying Past Quizzes" → Scale · Security · Schema · Continuous availability · Consistency · Partition tolerance · Quality of data.
Notice: Consistency + Availability + Partition tolerance = CAP (see §3.9).
